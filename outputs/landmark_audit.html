The model predicts 76 keypoints: SuperAnimal-Quadruped's 39 body points, unchanged, plus 37 added channels covering DogFLW's 46 facial landmarks. Nine of the 46 already existed in SuperAnimal and share their channel, which is why 46 landmarks need only 37 new outputs.

Part one

How it was built

01

The blocker: DeepLabCut cannot add keypoints

DeepLabCut ships "memory replay" for fine-tuning a SuperAnimal model without catastrophic forgetting. It structurally cannot add outputs — prepare_memory_replay maps every project bodypart back into SuperAnimal's 39-keypoint space, and PoseModel.build loads the pretrained head with a load_state_dict that requires exactly that many channels.

So the project generalises memory replay rather than calling it. Everything else — model construction, dataloading, augmentation, the training loop — is stock DeepLabCut.

02

Correspondence, derived from data rather than a figure

Rather than trusting a published landmark diagram, the pretrained model was run over all 4,335 DogFLW faces and each SuperAnimal keypoint matched to its nearest DogFLW landmark. A merge required mutual nearest-neighbour agreement and median separation under 5% of the face-bbox diagonal.

9 merged, 37 added → 76 outputs. The four antler keypoints were excluded from merging: on a dog the model puts antler_base on top of earbase, so they win the test by accident.

03

Head surgery, bit-exact on the original 39

The head is a single 1×1 ConvTranspose2d over HRNet's 32-channel map, so adding outputs means adding rows to a weight matrix. The 39 pretrained filters were copied verbatim; each new filter was initialised from its spatially nearest SuperAnimal keypoint plus 1e-3 noise.

That −1 is load-bearing: it makes the target generator set that channel's loss weight to zero, so training neither invents a label nor pushes the channel toward background. It is what keeps the body keypoints intact — measured drift stayed at 0.0000 at every stage.

One design decision underpins all of it: training crops are whole-dog detector boxes, not face boxes. The model is top-down, so at inference a detector supplies a whole-animal box and the pose head sees that crop. Training must match.

05

Two-phase training

Phase 1 freezes the 29.3M-parameter backbone and trains only the 2,508-parameter head — backward never reaches the backbone, which on CPU is the difference between hours and minutes. Phase 2 unfreezes HRNet's stage4 (21.2M parameters) at a much lower learning rate.

06

An experiment that failed, and cost two days

negative result

Sharpening the heatmap targets from σ=17 to σ=8 was predicted to raise precision on dense facial landmarks. It made accuracy worse.

NME went 0.0583 → 0.0630 over two days of CPU training. The likely mechanism only became clear later: with no sub-pixel decoding (step 08a), the decoded position quantises to the heatmap grid regardless of how sharp the peak is — so sharpening removes training signal without buying precision.

Its per-epoch curve also showed train loss going flat while test NME was still falling. Train loss is not a stopping signal on this project.

Training moved nose, muzzle and mouth by 30–47%. It moved the ears and skull top by 6–8% — statistically, barely at all. Three separate ideas were tested and ruled out as causes: resolution (tripling the face's pixel count moved ear error 4% while moving the other 30 landmarks 20%), geometry (a shape model on ears made them worse, 0.100 → 0.180), and breed identity (ear type explains 32% of the tips' scatter but 1.4% of the worst points').

08

Four fixes applied after the network — where the accuracy actually came from

Every remaining cause turned out to be fixable at inference. None required retraining.

a · the largest bug in the project

Keypoints were snapping to a 4-pixel grid, collapsing dense landmarks onto identical coordinates

HeatmapPredictor decodes each keypoint as torch.argmax over the 64×64 heatmap, then adds a learned offset from the locref map. These models were trained with generate_locref: false, so that offset is always None and every point lands on a cell centre.

Two landmarks peaking in the same cell therefore decode to byte-identical coordinates and render as one dot. Measured on video, 46 face channels occupied 16 distinct pixel positions. Fitting a parabola through each peak and its neighbours — standard HRNet/DARK post-processing — gives 44 of 46 distinct, plus ~7% better NME everywhere, because the rounding was real error and not just a display artefact.

b · ear NME 0.0882 → 0.0631, −28.5%

One channel serves both erect and floppy ears, so the model averages two geometries — and a split difference is a consistent error

The ear failure inverts by ear type, which is why it looked like noise in aggregate: the bias on ear_*_tip is 0.16 for erect ears and 0.78 for floppy, while on ear_*_outer_base it is 0.99 erect against 0.26 floppy. Averaged together those cancel to a 1.11× ratio.

A logistic classifier recovers ear type from the model's own predicted ear landmarks at 80% (51.8% majority baseline — nothing external needed at inference), and subtracting that type's mean residual improves every ear landmark by 12–42%. Predicted type matches an oracle type to within 0.0002, because misclassifications land between adjacent types whose biases are similar.

c · skull top 0.130 → 0.074, −43%

No local texture is not the same as undetermined

head_top_left/right sit on featureless fur — PCK@5% of 0.6%, which is chance. But the crown is implied by the rest of the head, so a ridge shape model fitted on the training split derives both from the CNN's own predictions of the 30 reliable landmarks.

The same trick fails on ears (0.100 → 0.180). An ear can be perked or flopped independently of the face, so geometry cannot predict it. Only landmarks that are globally determined and locally featureless qualify.

d · available, off by default

Two-pass head crop: better numbers, worse picture

Re-cutting the box so the face fills 55% of it (a whole-dog box on video gives ~25%) is worth ~18% NME on the reliable landmarks. It is off by default because it lowers confidence, and with sub-pixel decoding active it drops distinct face points from 44 to 25. Use it when measuring, not when looking.

What generalised

Three things worth carrying forward

1

An error that looks like noise in aggregate is often systematic once conditioned on the right variable

This produced three of the four fixes. UNRELIABLE in the codebase went from 14 landmarks, to 2, to empty — each time because a "structurally unlearnable" point turned out to have a consistent, subtractable error. Before concluding a landmark cannot be learned, check whether its error has a direction given something observable.

2

On this model, confidence runs opposite to accuracy

Across the crop-scale sweep confidence fell from 0.807 to 0.589 as accuracy improved. It is a visibility control, never a quality signal. Nothing should be gated or tuned by watching it rise — a gate built on it skipped the second pass on 0 of 20 frames that needed it.

3

Mean NME across all 46 landmarks is the wrong way to rank checkpoints here

It weights a garbage ear point exactly like an eye corner, and ignores confidence entirely — so it ranked the model that hides its failures below the one that displays them. Ranking on it also nearly shipped the model that draws 33 of 46 points instead of 46.

Part two

What could be done next

Ranked by expected value against effort. The first two are the ones I would actually do.

1

Retrain with generate_locref: true

effort: head surgery + one training run · expected: replaces a workaround with the real mechanism

Sub-pixel peak fitting recovers what argmax throws away, but it is inference-side estimation. A learned offset head is what the architecture is designed around, and it is the reason the σ=8 experiment failed — sharpening peaks buys nothing while the decode is quantised. With locref, sharper targets should finally pay off, so this unlocks a lever that is currently dead.

Cost: locref adds 2 output channels per keypoint, so the head grows 76 → 228 and needs another surgery pass; the new channels start untrained. Evaluate the forgetting metric, since it also retrains the original 39.

2

Split into two models — face and body, dual inference

effort: substantial · expected: ~10–20% on the 30 good landmarks, ~0 on ears

Worth doing for the simplification alone. It deletes the entire memory-replay apparatus — pseudo-labels, visibility masking, 76-channel head surgery — because the body model becomes stock SuperAnimal, never touched. The face model then trains on face crops natively instead of the whole-dog-crop compromise, and each gets its own crop size, σ and augmentation. The head box comes free from the body model's eye and nose keypoints, which are its best channels.

Be clear about what it will not fix: ear error is flat across a 3× crop range, so a better-scaled face model does not touch it.

3

Ear-type-conditioned output channels

effort: one training run · expected: attacks the cause the correction only patches

The bias correction is post-hoc — it subtracts an averaged error rather than preventing the averaging. Giving each ear landmark a channel per ear type (14 × 3 = 42 channels, negligible parameters on a 1×1 head) and supervising only the matching set would let the model learn two geometries instead of splitting the difference. The masking machinery already exists: visibility −1 zeroes a channel's loss weight, which is exactly what is needed.

At inference, select the channel set with the existing 80% classifier. Ceiling is unknown — the correction already recovers 28.5%, so the remaining headroom may be modest.

4

Carve out a real validation split

effort: an hour · expected: no accuracy gain, but every number becomes trustworthy

There is currently no validation set. Choosing a stopping epoch used the test split, which biases the reported figure. The differences were small enough that no epoch was cherry-picked, but any further tuning — and points 1 and 3 both involve tuning — needs a clean split first, or the results will not be defensible.

5

Video-domain augmentation

effort: a config change plus a training run · expected: unmeasured, plausibly significant

DogFLW is close-up portraits; the target domain is video — lower resolution, motion blur, the animal at distance. Nothing in training simulates that. Adding downscale, blur and JPEG-artefact augmentation is cheap and directly targets the gap between where the model was trained and where it is used. This has not been tested and should be measured before being believed.

6

Get the DogFLW landmark manual from the authors

effort: an email · expected: settles a question nothing else can

The landmark names in this project are inferred from the data — the mean face shape is near-perfectly bilaterally symmetric, giving 20 mirror pairs and 6 midline points, and the groups were read off that. Two independent cross-checks passed, but the real manual is behind a dead link shortener.

It also settles the one open question about the ear contour points: if ear_*_outer_mid is defined as "evenly spaced along the ear edge", then no model can recover it from pixels and the right treatment is to model the ear as a curve and sample along it, rather than as independent point detectors.

7

Fine-tune the detector

effort: moderate · expected: small, and partly obsolete

Stage 1 is stock SuperAnimal and was never touched. It is the reason the face arrives at ~25% of the crop on video instead of the ~56% the pose head trained on. A head-aware detector would remove the need for the two-pass crop entirely. Lower priority now that sub-pixel decoding has absorbed most of what the crop was compensating for.
